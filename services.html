Services

A proper barbershop, wherever the chair goes.

The same traditional service you’d get on the high street — adapted, paced and made safe for gentlemen living with dementia, disability or serious illness.

In the chair

What I offer

Haircuts

A proper cut, finished the way he has always had it. For residents in care, and for gentlemen at home who can no longer get to a barbershop.

Hot towel shaves

Warm towels, warm lather and the full traditional routine — performed with a foil shaver and a bladeless razor, so the experience is identical and the risk is nil.

Beard trims & shaping

Tidying, shaping and upkeep, at whatever length suits him. Often the quickest way to make a man look and feel like himself again.

Sensory & wellbeing sessions

Grooming built around comfort and recognition rather than the finished haircut — warm towels, familiar sounds, familiar photographs, and time to talk.

Regular care home visits

A standing monthly slot, roughly every four to five weeks, on the same sort of date each time. Routine is what makes this work.

One-off & ad hoc visits

A single visit to cover a gap, a new admission, a family occasion or a home you’d like to try before committing to a schedule.

Home visits

For gentlemen living with dementia at home, and for people whose disability or mobility makes leaving the house difficult or exhausting.

Special-needs-friendly sessions

One-to-one appointments in a quiet, controlled setting for anyone who finds a normal barbershop overwhelming — plus the group events below.

End-of-life & palliative grooming

Bedside shaving, washing and tidying for gentlemen receiving palliative care, so a family can see their husband, dad or grandad looking like himself.

A typical visit

How the day runs

The aim is a visit that feels reassuring to residents and completely undisruptive to staff.

  1. Arrive and sign in

    I sign in, greet the team and go through the day’s list, so I know who I’m seeing and whether anyone needs extra time or a gentler approach.

  2. Check in with staff

    A quick word about how each gentleman has been — any changes in mood, mobility or behaviour, and anyone who’d do better somewhere quieter.

  3. Set up the shop

    Equipment laid out in a calm, familiar space. Clean, safe and ready before the first gentleman sits down.

  4. Roughly twenty minutes each

    The visit runs best when a member of the team brings the next gentleman along and walks the last one back. It keeps the flow calm and gives me time to clean down between each one.

  5. Finish, check, hand back

    I check he’s comfortable, return him to staff with a quick word on how it went, and note anything worth knowing for next time.

  6. Clean down and confirm the next date

    Everything packed away, the area left exactly as I found it, and the next visit booked in — or the next few months block-booked — before I leave.

Adapting

Every gentleman, on his own terms

Dementia doesn’t present the same way twice, and it doesn’t present the same way on two consecutive days. I work with the man in front of me.

If he’s anxious

I keep everything steady and predictable, speak clearly and avoid sudden changes, so he always knows what is about to happen. No surprises, no sudden noise.

If he’s non-verbal

I rely on body language, eye contact and simple cues, and I ask staff or family what usually settles him. Not speaking is not the same as not communicating.

If he’s resistant to touch

Nothing is forced. I give him time, change my pace or my position, and if it still isn’t right I step away and come back later in the visit.

If he’s bed-bound

I set up safely at the bedside and keep the back short while leaving the rest exactly as he has always had it. It prevents matting and sweat soreness where he lies, without changing how he looks to his family.

Community

Special-needs-friendly events

A private hall or sports court, booked for the session, so nobody is queuing in a shop window. The space is private — but the event itself is open, lively and completely relaxed.

These are for people with autism, ADHD, learning disabilities, mobility needs, dementia, or anyone who finds a standard barbershop impossible. The point isn’t a silent, clinical room. The point is a judgement-free one.

Move about. Make noise. Stim. Go outside and come back in. Take as long as you need. Nobody is going to stare, and nobody is going to complain.

  • Plenty of time for each person, at whatever pace works on the day
  • Families can come and go as they need to
  • Every haircut adapted to what the individual can manage
  • Snacks, a charity raffle and simple activities alongside
  • No pressure, no embarrassment, and no awkward apologies

Pricing

Simple, and worked out per visit

Care home visits are priced as a half day or a full day rather than per haircut, so a home always knows exactly what a visit costs before I arrive. There is a minimum number of gentlemen per visit, and a flexible rate for homes that fall between the two.

Home visits are quoted separately depending on where you are and what’s needed. There is no travel charge for local visits.

Who I visit at home

If getting to a barbershop has stopped being possible

Not being able to manage a barbershop any more is one of those small losses that stings more than it should. It doesn’t have to be one.

Living with dementia

At home, in familiar surroundings, with nothing rushed. If today isn’t a good day, we work around it — or I come back another time.

Disability or limited mobility

Wheelchair, chair or bed — I set up safely wherever he is most comfortable, rather than moving him to suit me.

Palliative & end-of-life care

Bedside shaving, washing and tidying, handled gently and without fuss, so he looks and feels like himself for the people around him.

What to expect

A quiet hour, and no performance required

Before I come, I’ll ask a few simple things — how he usually has his hair, what tends to unsettle him, what tends to settle him, and whether there’s a time of day that works better.

Then I arrive, set up somewhere comfortable, and let him take the lead. If he wants to talk, we talk. If he doesn’t, that’s fine too. Nobody has to make conversation and nobody has to apologise for anything.

When it’s done, everything is cleaned up and taken away with me. You won’t be hoovering after I leave.

  • No live blade is ever used — the hot towel shave is done with a foil shaver and a bladeless razor
  • Nothing is ever forced. Refusal is an answer, and I respect it
  • His usual haircut, kept the way he has always had it
  • Enhanced DBS checked and fully insured
  • No travel charge for local visits
  • You’re welcome to stay in the room, or to take the hour for yourself

One visit, a week before Christmas

He was crying because he wanted to say thank you and couldn’t get the words out.

I was asked to visit a gentleman in Telford who was receiving end-of-life care at home. He had a brain tumour, couldn’t move from his bed and could no longer speak. His family had been told he’d be lucky to see the new year, and they wanted him to feel normal and comfortable for one last Christmas at home.

With his family helping to lift him, I gave him a full hot towel shave, cleaned up the back of his head where it had been missed, and soaked a towel in lemon to wash him from his collarbone up. Fresh, tidy, and looking like himself again.

When we finished, he cried. His wife explained he was upset because he wanted to thank me and couldn’t.

That visit is why I do this. It isn’t barbering. It’s dignity, and normality, and giving a family something good at a time when everything else is being taken away.

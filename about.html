About

Chris Floyd, founder

Twenty-three years behind the chair. A year inside care homes. And a nan who has lived with vascular dementia for eighteen of them.

Why I started this

I didn’t start The Dementia Barber Co. because I wanted another job or another business. I started it because something was missing.

Since October 2025 I’d been giving my spare time to another company, visiting care homes and supporting gentlemen living with dementia. I was grateful that they trusted me to work in my own way — it gave me room to use my instincts and deliver something that felt calm and dignified rather than processed. What started as a few visits opened my eyes to something much bigger.

I saw how a single grooming session could change a man’s entire day. I also saw the opposite: residents becoming stressed, confused or unsettled during something that should have been one of the calmest parts of their week, because the person holding the clippers didn’t understand the condition, the behaviour or the need. It didn’t sit right with me then and it still doesn’t.

A haircut isn’t just a haircut. It’s identity. It’s routine. It’s reassurance. It’s a moment where someone feels like themselves again.

Over time, with new routines, personalised techniques and consistent monthly visits, I built trust with residents, staff and families. That trust is what lets me give these gentlemen something genuinely worth looking forward to: a predictable visit, a familiar face, and a safe hour to open up and walk away feeling like the man who used to leave the barbershop forty years ago.

These men built the communities we live in. They worked the jobs that shaped our towns, raised families and lived lives full of graft and pride. They deserve to spend their final years being appreciated for it — not rushed through a haircut by someone who doesn’t know them.

My nan

Dementia has been part of my life for a long time. My nan has lived with vascular dementia for eighteen years. She was a huge part of bringing me up and she always put me first. Growing up around her taught me patience, empathy, and how much dignity matters when someone’s world becomes hard to navigate.

Everything I’ve learned in care homes has shown me I can take what she taught me and use it at a much larger scale. My nan taught me compassion. The gentlemen I work with taught me what consistency and patience can achieve. The homes I’ve worked in showed me how badly this is needed.

The moment I decided to go out on my own

A gentleman in his eighties, at a home in Birmingham, changed everything for me. During one of his appointments he told me how low he was feeling, and that he wanted it all to be over. I took that home with me and it stayed with me.

Since then, whenever I’m passing or I have a day off, I call in and sit with him so he knows someone is thinking about him. He loves vintage cars, so I bought a model kit and we build a bit more of it each time I visit. He’s a genuinely good man, and he’s finally going home soon. He’s asked whether I’ll keep visiting until he leaves, and I’ve told him I’ll carry on cutting his hair once he’s back at home.

That was the moment I realised I could make a bigger difference doing this properly, on my own terms, for more gentlemen who need it.

My background

I have twenty-three years of professional hairdressing experience. I started on the unisex side of the industry and won Trainee Hairdresser of the Year in 2008 while I was still training. I moved into barbering in 2011 and have been there ever since — including the past twelve months working hands-on inside care homes, supporting gentlemen living with dementia.

That year has been spent adapting grooming techniques, communication and equipment to keep residents safe, calm and comfortable, on a frequent and consistent basis.

On qualifications — straight answer

I don’t currently hold a formal dementia qualification. What I have is twenty-three years of barbering and a year of regular, hands-on work with residents living with dementia, which is where my dementia-aware practice has been built and tested. I’d rather tell you that up front than dress it up. I’m Enhanced DBS checked and fully insured, and every document your compliance team needs is available on request.

In the press

My work at a care home in Leighton Buzzard was covered by the local paper while I was visiting on behalf of a previous company.

Non-negotiable

What I won’t compromise on

  • No live blade I say this from the start. I will not put a blade on skin that may be fragile, unpredictable or affected by blood-thinning medication. The hot towel shave uses a foil shaver, warm lather and a razor with the blade removed. Same experience, same finish, no risk.
  • Nobody gets rushed I will not force a service or push through distress to get down a list. If a gentleman is anxious, confused or says no, I step back and come again later.
  • No corners on hygiene Infection control is the same on the last resident of the day as it is on the first. That doesn’t change because the diary is tight.
  • No unsafe environments I won’t work somewhere chaotic or unsafe, or where staff aren’t willing to support the process. It isn’t fair on the residents.
  • Nobody is a task Dignity, preference and personal boundaries come before the schedule, every single time.
